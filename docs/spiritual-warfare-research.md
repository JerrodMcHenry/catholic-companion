## 2. Proposed topics (consolidated, 10 new; awaiting approval)

All in a new category `spiritual-warfare`, shown as section 8 of Learn the Faith, using the existing category and topic pages. No new route or component is needed.

1. `angels`: What are angels? Includes guardian angels, Michael, Gabriel, and Raphael, the traditional hierarchies (labeled as theological tradition), and devotion. CCC 328–336, 350–352; Tobit 12; Daniel 10; Matthew 18:10; Hebrews 1:14; Aquinas, ST I qq. 50, 108–113.
2. `satan-and-demons`: Is Satan real? Covers who Satan is, the rebellion (only what is revealed: CCC 391–392), the limits on his power (CCC 395), why demons are creatures and not rivals of God, why Christians need not fear, and whether demons can read thoughts (Aquinas, ST I q. 57 a. 4, labeled theological opinion). John 8:44; 1 Peter 5:8–9; 1 John 3:8; Revelation 12; Job 1–2.
3. `spiritual-warfare`: What is spiritual warfare? Christ's victory first (Colossians 2:13–15; John 16:33; 1 John 4:4); the world, the flesh, and the devil (traditional triad); the armor of God (Ephesians 6:10–18); grace and freedom; ordinary struggle before anything extraordinary. CCC 409, 2015, 2725, 2850–2854.
4. `temptation`: Is being tempted a sin? Covers temptation versus consent (CCC 2847); thought, desire, consent, and action; the conditions for mortal sin (CCC 1857–1860); unwanted and intrusive thoughts (not sin, plus when to seek help for OCD or scrupulosity); occasions of sin; habits; the passions as morally neutral in themselves (CCC 1762–1770); intellect and will in plain language. James 1:12–15; 1 Corinthians 10:13; Matthew 4:1–11 (CCC 538–540); Matthew 26:41. This absorbs the proposed "human nature" material instead of a separate page.
5. `capital-sins`: What are the seven deadly sins? Each sin with its opposing virtue, everyday examples, and practical steps (including lust and pornography, anger and unforgiveness, envy and comparison, sloth and spiritual apathy). It distinguishes capital vices from mortal sin. CCC 1865–1869; Galatians 5:16–26; Cassian, Institutes (New Advent); Aquinas.
6. `discernment`: What is spiritual discernment? Consolation and desolation (Ignatius, Spiritual Exercises 313–336, public domain on CCEL), prudence and humility, spiritual direction, and the explicit safeguard not to read emotions, dreams, coincidences, or bodily sensations as signs of demonic activity.
7. `sacramentals`: What are sacramentals? Covers sacraments versus sacramentals (CCC 1667–1679); holy water, the crucifix, medals (including the St. Benedict Medal and the Miraculous Medal), the Brown Scapular, and blessings; superstition; and the St. Michael prayer (Leo XIII). Claims about scapular and medal promises must be verified against Church documents in Phase D.
8. `occult-practices`: Why does the Church warn against the occult? Covers divination, astrology, tarot, magic, mediums and spiritism, Ouija boards, and superstition (CCC 2110–2117, 2138; Deuteronomy 18:10–12; Acts 19:19). Includes compassionate next steps for those who took part (Confession, talking to a priest), with no assumption of "attachment," and no instructions.
9. `exorcism-and-deliverance`: What is exorcism? Covers canon 1172 and the bishop's permission; CCC 1673, including "Illness, especially psychological illness, is a very different matter"; the 1985 CDF letter (laypeople may not use the exorcism formula or question demons, but should pray to be delivered from evil); when to contact a parish priest; and what to avoid. Categories such as oppression, obsession, and infestation come from exorcists' pastoral writings; they are not magisterial diagnostic categories. They would be mentioned only as such, if at all.
10. `daily-spiritual-life`: How do I build a daily spiritual life? Covers morning offering, the day, temptation, evening examen, and what to do regularly or as needed, clearly separating Church obligations (Sunday Mass, annual Confession, Easter Communion, fast and abstinence days) from optional devotions. No routine is imposed and nothing is tracked.

Existing topics to strengthen lightly: `virtues` (humility, patience, chastity, generosity, diligence, linked to `capital-sins`), `our-father` (the petition "deliver us from evil," CCC 2850–2854), `confession` (link to `temptation`), and `how-to-pray` (link to `daily-spiritual-life`).

Homepage: one compact Spiritual Warfare card linking to `/category/spiritual-warfare`, added only when the section exists.

## 3. Sources verified in this phase

- **Canon 1172** (vatican.va): exorcism only by a priest with the local ordinary's "special and express permission."
- **CDF letter Inde ab aliquot annis (1985)** (vatican.va): the faithful may not use the formula of exorcism against Satan; those without the faculty may not lead meetings in which "demons are questioned directly and their identity sought to be known"; the faithful should still pray to be delivered from evil (Matthew 6:13).
- **Catechism** (vatican.va), paragraphs read: 328, 336 (angels); 391, 395 (Satan's fall and limits); 538 (Jesus' temptation); 1673 (exorcism; psychological illness is a medical matter); 1767–1768 (passions neither good nor evil); 1866 (capital sins, following Cassian and Gregory the Great); 2116–2117 (divination, magic); 2847, 2851–2852 (temptation versus consent; the Evil One).
- **Accessible for Phase D** (links checked, contents not yet read): Aquinas, ST I q. 50 and q. 114 (New Advent); Cassian (New Advent 3507); Francis de Sales, Introduction to the Devout Life (CCEL); Ignatius, Spiritual Exercises (CCEL); Rossetti's Saint Michael Center site (catholicexorcism.org).

## 5. Doctrinal distinctions to keep

- **Doctrine:** angels exist (CCC 328); Satan and the demons are fallen angels, created good (CCC 391); their power is limited and permitted by providence (CCC 395); Christ has conquered (Colossians 2:15; CCC 2853).
- **Discipline:** canon 1172; the 1985 CDF norms.
- **Traditional theology:** angelic hierarchies (Pseudo-Dionysius, Aquinas); the world, the flesh, and the devil; whether demons know thoughts (Aquinas: not directly).
- **Pastoral literature (not doctrine):** categories of diabolic influence; claims about curses, "rights," and generational spirits.
- **Spiritual counsel:** Ignatian rules, de Sales on temptation.

## 6. Pastoral concerns

- Ordinary struggle comes first; extraordinary phenomena are mentioned only to direct people to a priest, with medical evaluation.
- Never imply that mental illness, addiction, intrusive thoughts, nightmares, or sleep paralysis are evidence of demons.
- Recommend pastoral and professional care together.
- No techniques "guaranteeing" protection.
- Watch for scrupulosity: temptation is not sin, and unwanted thoughts are not consent.
- Fear-free tone; the homepage card stays compact.
